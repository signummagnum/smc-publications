# How to Verify an Online Degree: An Evidence-First Checklist

**Version:** 1.0  
**Published:** August 25, 2026  
**Publisher:** [Signum Magnum College](https://smceducation.com/)  
**License:** [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

## Purpose

A polished website, a ranking badge, or the word *accredited* is not enough to establish the status of an institution or degree. This open educational resource provides a repeatable process for checking the exact provider, program, awarding body, qualification level, delivery mode, and recognition question through authoritative records.

It is designed for prospective learners, academic advisers, employers, admissions teams, and researchers. It does not decide whether a particular qualification will be accepted for employment, immigration, a regulated profession, title use, or further study. Those are purpose-specific decisions made by the competent receiving authority.

## Learning outcomes

After using this checklist, a reader should be able to:

1. distinguish provider licensing from program accreditation;
2. match a marketed program to the exact official record;
3. separate qualification-framework level from purpose-specific recognition;
4. identify common warning signs; and
5. retain a clear evidence log for a consequential decision.

## Step 1 — Establish the exact identity

Record the following before evaluating any claim:

- full institutional name;
- legal operator, where published;
- official website domain;
- country and regulatory jurisdiction;
- physical or registered address;
- awarding institution named for the qualification; and
- any acronym, former name, partner, franchise, or recruiting entity that could cause confusion.

Do not assume that organizations sharing an acronym, brand fragment, recruiting relationship, or historical connection are the same legal or academic institution.

## Step 2 — Verify the provider

Use the responsible public regulator's register, not a badge copied onto the provider's website.

Confirm:

- the provider appears under the exact name;
- its license or registration number;
- its current status;
- the category of provider it is licensed to operate as;
- the jurisdiction covered by that authorization; and
- whether the register states limits on level, delivery, location, or scope.

Save the direct register URL, access date, and a copy or screenshot where appropriate. A generic regulator homepage is weaker evidence than the exact provider record.

## Step 3 — Verify the exact program

Provider licensing and program accreditation are related but separate facts. Match all material details:

- exact qualification title;
- awarding institution;
- qualification type;
- national and international framework level, if applicable;
- credit value or study volume;
- delivery mode and approved location;
- language, where relevant;
- current offering or expiry status; and
- any special conditions in the official record.

Do not treat a short course, certificate, diploma, bachelor's degree, master's degree, professional doctorate, and research doctorate as interchangeable.

## Step 4 — Interpret qualification frameworks correctly

A qualifications framework describes the complexity and learning outcomes of a qualification. It supports comparison across education systems; it does not automatically decide:

- admission to another institution;
- access to a regulated profession;
- immigration or visa treatment;
- public-service eligibility;
- use of a protected professional or academic title;
- employer acceptance; or
- automatic recognition in every country.

Treat any claim that a framework level guarantees all of these outcomes as a warning sign.

## Step 5 — Ask the purpose-specific recognition question

Identify the body that will make the actual decision for your intended use. Depending on the purpose, this may be:

- a university admissions office;
- a national recognition information center;
- a professional regulator;
- an immigration authority;
- a public-service commission;
- an employer; or
- another competent authority.

Ask that body about the exact institution and qualification. Retain its current written requirements and response. An institution cannot guarantee a third party's future decision.

## Step 6 — Check the evidence trail

Prefer sources in this order:

1. the responsible public regulator;
2. the official qualifications database;
3. the awarding institution's governing documents and official site;
4. recognized international institutional databases;
5. independent reporting and scholarly sources; and
6. commercial directories, aggregators, forums, and social posts.

Lower-ranked sources may help discover a claim, but they should not replace the authoritative record.

## Warning signs

Pause and investigate further when you encounter:

- accreditation described without naming the accrediting authority;
- a logo or badge that does not link to an exact official record;
- a program title that differs materially from the register;
- a partner presented as though it were the awarding institution;
- an acronym used to blur separate organizations;
- framework-level language presented as a worldwide guarantee;
- pressure to pay before the awarding body and terms are clear;
- missing or inconsistent addresses, legal names, or domains;
- claims that cannot be reconciled with the regulator; or
- promises of guaranteed employment, salary, immigration, title use, or further-study admission.

## Decision rule

Proceed only when the provider, exact program, awarding institution, and intended use can be reconciled across authoritative records. If material details conflict, rely on the responsible public authority and obtain a written explanation before applying, paying, or presenting the qualification for a consequential purpose.
